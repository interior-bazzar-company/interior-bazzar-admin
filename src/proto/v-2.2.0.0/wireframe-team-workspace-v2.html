What this is, and what it decides

Twelve screens, each one marked live, extends something shipped, or new. Two entities here are genuinely new — Agreement and Resource. Everything else is a face, a column, a small record, or a read of another module. The board's Group by control is really wired, because TM-AD-15 is the decision most likely to be argued about and a screenshot cannot settle it.

The three rules no screen here may break
  1. An absence is the lack of a row. absent, unclosed and delayed are derived at read against asOf and never stored.
  2. isLate IS stored, written once at open against that member's own dayStartsAt. Changing policy tomorrow must not make last month late.
  3. Milestone and target progress is derived, never typed.

TM-AD-15 Status stays the default, and it is the only draggable axis. It is the one axis whose columns carry a transition table, so dragging between them means something. On every other axis the columns are a read-only grouping: you cannot drag a card from Meera's column into Arjun's and have that mean "reassign", because a reassignment needs a reason and an audit row, and a drag has neither.

§3.3

Calendar face — what /work opens on

/work?face=calendarnew · default face

Google Calendar is UX inspiration only, and the layout is now taken with it: a left rail beside a month grid. Taken — the rail, Create at the top of it, the mini month, Today, the month grid, the coloured chip, click-a-day-to-create, keyboard paging. Not taken — overlapping event layout, all-day versus timed lanes, recurrence, invitations, drag-to-resize. The rail is not a calendar widget: it belongs to the module and stays on all four faces — switch to the board or the timeline and it is still there.

A multi-day task appears on every day it spans, not only its due date. startDate has been on the model since v1 and neither shipped face ever used it. An item that shows only on its last day is a deadline list, not a schedule.
TM-AD-28 …but a quarter-long item is not an event on ninety-two days. Drawn the naïve way, this month is unreadable: the two targets and Enquiry response under 4 hours run from July to 30 September, so every single day of September printed the same three chips and pushed the day's actual tasks into +4 more. The rule is therefore: a task of a week or less draws on every day it spans; anything longer, and every milestone and target, draws exactly twice — ▸ starts and ▪ due. The long ones are not lost, they are in the rail with a progress bar, which is the right shape for a commitment that has no single day. This was found by rendering the month, not by reasoning about it.
Week view is the same grid with seven columns and rows by item — no hour gutter. Nothing in this module has a start and end time except attendance, which has its own strip. An hour grid would be mostly empty, and it is the largest piece of net-new UI in the plan for the least return.
TM-AD-25 The rail is the module's shell, not the calendar's sidebar. It carries Create, the mini month, This month, Progress and Today, and it does not move when the face does — progress that vanishes the moment somebody switches to the board is a progress indicator nobody trusts. Below 1240px it collapses, which is the breakpoint the panel already uses. It is drawn on §3.2 and §3.4 too, from the same function and the same five queries, so this is one component and not three sidebars that drift. What it deliberately does not hold is the filters. Google's rail lists calendars to tick; ours would have to list members, kinds and tags — and a filter that survives a face switch is how somebody loses half their board without noticing. Filters stay in each face's own toolbar. The rail holds only what is true whichever face is open.
TM-AD-26 One Create control, three kinds. + Create ▾ opens Task · Milestone · Target and all three open the same form with kind prefilled — because they are one WorkItem with a kind (TM-AD-15's premise), and a target only adds two fields to it, targetValue and targetUnit. Three buttons would become three forms, then three validators, then three lists. The + New item button is gone from every face toolbar: one entry point, and it is always in the same place.
TM-AD-27 Every number in the rail is derived, and drawing it caught three that were not. A milestone bar is completed children ÷ total children; a target bar is currentValue ÷ targetValue. Nothing is stored, and nothing is typed. The seed carried a typed progressPct on each milestone — Enquiry response under 4 hours said 60%, Support handover document said 33% — and against their own children both are 0%, because not one task under either is complete. The typed field is gone from the seed. That gap is the feature: a rail that reads 0 of 4 is the only thing that will make anyone close a task. One more number was reconciled on the way: Onboard 60 businesses was drawn at 38 / 60 here and stands at 47 / 60 in work.json, which is the file of record — the screen was copying a stale number.
Today is a summary, and it links to the page that owns the day. Overdue first, then due today, then the next three days, capped — overdue above today, because a day list that hides what is already late is how work goes missing. Ticking a box completes the item in place; everything else — reordering, rescheduling, the full list — is /team/me?tab=work (§3.13), which already exists in this plan and already owns the member's own work. The rail summarises it and never becomes a second one.
Progress is two sections: Mine, then My team. Team is reportsTo, one level, never transitive — TM-AD-13 — so a member with no reports simply never sees the second heading, and nothing on the rail is a per-person figure. TM-OD-11 holds: a bar against a milestone counts its children; a bar against a person would be the score this module refuses to compute.
Being the default costs one thing and it has to be paid: the empty month. The board opens with 22 cards no matter the week; a month with nothing dated in it opens blank, and a blank landing screen reads as a broken panel. The empty state therefore says how many items exist but carry no date, and links straight to the board — 22 items · 6 with no date ▸ open the board. That is also the cheapest nudge towards dating work that the module will ever get.
The face is remembered per member, and the default only decides the first visit. Someone who works the board all day should not be sent back to a month grid every morning — ?face= stays the source of truth, the last face is remembered locally, and /work with neither falls to the calendar.
TM-OD-08 Drag and drop stays out, reaffirmed. Reschedule is a date field in the drawer. A silent drag leaves no reason behind it.
§3.4

Milestone timeline

/work?face=timelinenew

One lane per target and per milestone — the containment tree work.json already stores, target ▸ milestone ▸ task — with the tasks inside each one drawn from startDate to dueDate. The axis is the work, not the worker. The question this face answers is "is this milestone going to land"; who is busy is the board's Assignee axis (§3.2) and the member's own dashboard (§3.13).

Group: Milestone ▾ scope: targets · milestones · their tasks
◆ dashed = the lane's own committed window solid = a task, tone is its status delayed ╌╌▶ follows link click a bar → the drawer
⚠
TM-AD-24 The lanes are milestones, not members. A lane per person is a productivity chart whatever it is called: it invites the reading "Meera has four bars and Rahul has two", which nothing in the module justifies — there is no estimate field, so a bar's length is a date range and not an amount of work, and TM-OD-11 forbids scoring a person anyway. A lane per milestone is a delivery chart, where every bar is evidence for or against one dated commitment somebody made. Member load keeps two honest homes — Group by → Assignee on the board, and §3.13 — and the assignee still rides on every bar as initials, because "who is this waiting on" is a fair question to ask of a task.
Progress fill and leave bands left with the member lanes. A fill needs a per-task percentage nobody types, and an approved leave band says something real on a person's row and nothing at all on a milestone's. What a lane states instead is countable and already stored: n of m tasks done and the milestone's own dates. Leave stays on the calendar and on §3.7.
An item with no startDate cannot be drawn, so it is listed under the grid instead of being given an invented start. That is the honest state; a bar beginning at a date nobody chose is a lie the chart tells confidently. Six open items are in that state below, and that count is the number this face exists to shrink.
Three things this view must not become, each the usual way a timeline rots. Not a Gantt with dependencies that gate — follows draws the connector and nothing more. Not a resource planner — no capacity bar, no allocation percentage; the module has no estimate field and inventing one to fill a chart is how estimates get born. Not a ranking — TM-OD-11 stands: show the numbers, sort by any column, compute no score. The milestone axis is what keeps the third one true by construction: there is no member row left to rank.
TM-R-13 Timeline plus calendar is more net-new UI than the whole of v1's work face. Build C before D, month before week, and share one date-grid primitive. If D slips, the calendar alone answers the brief's headline — and it is now the face the module lands on, which is the other reason C cannot slip.

Pay and documents are hidden from a senior by default, and that is not an oversight. A reporting line does not imply access to somebody's pay. It is a separate grant, held by whoever the business decides — usually two people, and usually not the person's manager.
TM-AD-22 Amends TM-AD-21, which had a hole in it. The two entities stay separate regardless — TM-AD-18 is about direction, permission and retention, and none of those change because two sections share a tab.
A nudge must never leak what a tab hides. Switch to their senior and the Needs you block loses two rows. The unsigned NDA and the two missing documents are gone — not greyed, not "no access", gone — because the Documents tab is absent for that viewer and a row saying "1 agreement unsigned" would announce the existence of a document the same screen just refused to show. Everything derived from a hidden surface inherits its scope.
The clock is read-only here, and the actions stay in the topbar. The panel would otherwise have three End the day buttons — topbar strip, /attendance, and this header — and three buttons over one open day is two chances for the UI to disagree with itself mid-request. The dashboard states the day; /attendance and the topbar change it.
TM-OD-31 One grant is spelled two ways and they must be reconciled before Phase G. The v1 document calls it people-docs.view; §5 of this one calls it team.resource.view and adds team.agreement.send / revoke. If both ship, the panel has two answers to "may I open this person's PAN card", and the loose one gets handed out casually. Recommendation: one shared read verb — the sensitivity of an offer letter and an Aadhaar scan is identical — and separate write verbs, because sending an agreement and accepting an upload are genuinely different acts.
TM-OD-32 What happens to the member drawer that ships today? Once this route exists, the same facts render in two places, which is the drift v1 §3.4 warned about in its own first line. Recommendation: the drawer becomes a launcher. It keeps identity, status, roles and the four actions it already owns — edit, roles, send credentials, delete — and every informational block moves here, behind an Open dashboard ▸. One place per fact; the drawer stays the fast path from the roster.
Six tabs, and the sixth is the one to cut first. If this reads as crowded on a real member, Reports folds into Overview — the plan and the EOD are already cards there, and a Reports tab is really their history. That is the next fold, and it is cheaper than the Documents one because nothing new lives behind it.
